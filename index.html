Programming has always felt natural to me.

I tend to think in logic and structure instinctively, often visualising solutions and code flow before writing anything down. This allows me to quickly understand unfamiliar codebases, reason about intent, and adapt to different styles, even when they're unconventional or incomplete.

This way of thinking has also shaped how I explain concepts to others, helping me break down complex ideas and understand different problem-solving approaches.

How I Build Interfaces

My skill set has grown through a mix of required and self-driven learning. Some technologies were introduced through academic or professional projects, while others I chose deliberately out of curiosity and a desire to explore new ways of building interfaces.

I learn best by applying tools in real contexts like experimenting, iterating, and refining solutions until both the logic and the interaction feel solid. I'm particularly motivated by challenges that involve reasoning about state, data flow, and user interaction rather than purely visual implementation.
